# Skill: standalone shorts

The best moment of a video is not automatically a good short. A good short still makes sense with
the rest of the video removed. All three tests are required:

1. Starts clean: the first sentence leans on nothing said before it ("And...", "So that's why...", "he" with no one named).
2. Has a turn: a claim followed by what complicates, proves or answers it. A claim alone is a quote, not a clip.
3. Ends on a line: a finished sentence that lands (the answer, the reveal, the punchline, the laugh), never a trail-off or a setup for what comes next.

Prefer a clip whose last line sets up its first, so a replay feels intended (a loop). Never fake one.

What a stranger needs is inside the clip: who "he" is, what "this" points at. If the words point at
something on screen ("look at this drawing"), the picture must be there too.

Never stitch a turn from another part of the video into a single-moment clip; that is a story clip,
and it must keep every speaker's meaning.

Never invent or round numbers, names or results that were not said.
