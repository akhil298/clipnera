# Skill: motion graphics

A graphic earns its place only when it shows something the viewer cannot already see or hear
clearly: a list read out, a figure that is the point, a name the viewer may not know, a call to action.
Graphics that decorate are noise; fewer, correct graphics beat many.

- Evidence: every word and number on a graphic was said in the clip, in its word range. Never invent, round or "improve" a figure.
- Value: a list needs at least three items of one kind said in a row; a passing number ("two minutes ago") is not a figure.
- Already visible: if the thing is on screen (the drawing being reacted to, a product in hand), do not label it.
- Tone: match the clip's mood. Mock anger, roasting and teasing that gets laughs is funny, not serious; a sad or serious clip gets no jokey stickers.
- Most clips have no list and no call to action. Report nothing rather than stretch.
