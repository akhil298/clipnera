# Skill: hooks

A viewer decides in the first one to three seconds. The opening line must match what the clip
delivers, open a question the viewer cannot answer yet, and make it believable the answer is coming.
A line that only summarises the clip gives the ending away.

Shapes that work, and what each needs:
- Numbers: a real figure, a counted list, a deadline. Only figures actually said in the clip.
- Proof: a result, a before/after, "I tried it". The proof must be in the clip itself.
- Tension: a mistake, a warning, a reversal of common belief, a claim that sounds impossible. The clip must resolve it.
- Promise: a reveal, a countdown, a "the best/worst", a comparison. The payoff must land inside the clip.
- Address: a direct question to the viewer, insider knowledge, the start of a story. It must be the viewer's question, not the speaker's.

Judge a candidate on five things and let the weakest decide:
- Concrete: names, numbers, places beat hype words ("insane", "crazy").
- About the viewer: "you" early beats "I" or "we".
- Stakes: something to lose, gain or get wrong.
- Open: it raises a question; it must not answer itself ("...because...").
- Speakable: about 5-15 words, sayable in three seconds.

Never write a new hook line. Choose among lines that were said. Never invent a number to make a line stronger.
For Hindi, Telugu and mixed speech, judge the shapes in the language spoken ("sabse bada", "mistake chesthe").
